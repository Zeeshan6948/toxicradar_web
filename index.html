Exploitative Leadership Detection — Complete Guide

Core Mission

Empowering Leaders with Psychological Safety & Constructive Communication

Exploitative Leadership Detection (ELD) is an AI-powered workplace communication coaching and organizational intelligence platform. It analyzes drafted messages, real-time writing, and historical mailbox archives to identify, explain, and de-escalate exploitative, self-centered, or manipulative communication patterns before they damage team trust.

⚡
Why This Tool Exists

In fast-paced, high-stakes environments, well-intentioned leaders often slip into anxiety-inducing pressure, excessive self-referencing, or subtle guilt-tripping. These patterns silently erode psychological safety, trigger burnout, and drive top performers away. ELD provides an objective, private, and constructive feedback loop.

🔬
Academic Foundation (ELTC Model)

Unlike generic sentiment analysis tools that only categorize text as positive or negative, ELD is grounded in the validated Exploitative Leadership in Teams & Corporations (ELTC) research framework (Schmid et al.), diagnosing 5 distinct dimensions of managerial behavior.

🛡️
Real-Time Pre-Send Coaching

Draft emails with confidence. ELD provides real-time collaborative autocomplete, pronoun bias detection ("I/Me" vs. "We/Team"), live warning guardrails, and 1-click constructive rewrite suggestions before you click send.

📊
Departmental & Longitudinal Audits

Upload Outlook PST sent archives or spreadsheet logs to assess communication trends over days, weeks, months, or years, verifying whether coaching interventions are successfully fostering psychological safety.

1
Drafting & Real-Time Coaching ("New Message+")

Navigate to New Message+ in the sidebar to compose or paste an email draft:

  • ✨ Non-Exploitative Inline Autocomplete: Pause typing for 350ms to see collaborative continuations. Press Tab or → to accept, or Esc to dismiss.
  • ⚖️ Egoistic Pronoun Bias Detection: If your draft over-indexes on self-credit ("I completed", "my project"), an alert banner displays the exact pronoun split. Click Apply Team Framing to automatically convert text to shared team ownership.
  • 🚨 Live Warning Toggle: Turn on Live Warning above the editor for continuous real-time tone monitoring as you type.
  • 🔍 Send for Analysis: Generates a full 5-dimension score breakdown, highlights risky phrases, and provides an actionable Suggested Rewrite that can be applied with one click.
2
Auditing Historical Archives & Outlook PST ("Overview")

Navigate to Overview to audit past organizational communications:

  • 📁 Import Sent Archives: Click Choose PST File to parse an Outlook archive or upload existing Excel logs.
  • ☁️ Persistent Firebase Storage: All analyzed records, dimension scores, and balance factors survive server restarts and inactivity sleep cycles.
  • 📑 Filter by Risk Tier: Click the Negative, Neutral, or Positive headline badges to filter the email summary table.
  • 🔎 Verbatim Evidence Inspection: Click Details on any email to view verbatim quotes, score ratings, and coaching notes for each of the 5 dimensions.
3
Targeted Leadership Coaching (Left Sidebar)

Turn detected communication risks into measurable skill development:

  • 🎓 Contextual Recommendations: The left sidebar automatically surfaces coaching modules (Servant Leadership, Psychological Safety, Collaborative Influence, Autonomy Support) based on detected patterns.
  • 💡 Interactive Learning Modal: Click Review Module → to study side-by-side Before/After email comparisons and actionable daily leadership habits.
Visualization Guide

How to Interpret Metrics, Charts, and Analytics Across ToxicRadar

Every visualization in the platform is purposefully designed to give leaders and HR coaches actionable insight into workplace psychological safety and communication dynamics.

Overview Dashboard
1. Headline KPI Status Cards (Negative, Neutral, Positive %)

Located at the top of the Overview view, these three KPI badges summarize the health distribution across all analyzed emails:

🔴 Negative % (High Risk)

What it measures: Percentage of messages scoring $\ge 4$ or flagged for critical exploitative behaviors.

Action: Click this card to immediately filter the table below to review all high-risk communications.

🟡 Neutral % (Borderline)

What it measures: Percentage of messages scoring exactly 3. Functional and transactional, but often lacking collaborative warmth or psychological safety reassurance.

🟢 Positive % (Constructive)

What it measures: Percentage of messages scoring 1 or 2 (respectful, collaborative, and autonomy-supportive).

Goal: High-trust organizations aim to maintain Positive % above 80%.

Reports View
2. Dimension Score Breakdown (0–100 Quality Scale)

Evaluates communications across the 5 validated ELTC dimensions on a standardized 0 to 100 Communication Quality Scale:

Scale Formula: Quality Score = ((5 - average_dimension_score) / 4) * 100
100 Points: Flawless servant leadership with zero exploitative markers.
< 50 Points: Severe exploitative behavior requiring immediate coaching.

Why it matters: An overall toxicity score alone cannot diagnose the problem. The dimension breakdown pinpoints whether the root cause is Exerting Pressure during deadlines, Genuine Egoistic Behaviors in status updates, or Undermining Development in feedback.

Reports View
3. Time-Series Histograms & Multi-Series Trends (Daily, Monthly, Yearly)

Tracks communication trends longitudinally across selectable timeframes (Today, 7 Days, 30 Days, 90 Days, Total History):

  • Message Volume Mode ("count"): Displays total emails sent per day, week, or month, stacked by Negative (Red), Neutral (Yellow), and Positive (Green) volume.
  • High Risk % Mode ("riskPercent"): Plots the percentage of toxic emails over time. Allows leadership coaches to evaluate whether training and awareness are driving toxic communication down week-over-week.
  • Chart Type Switcher: Switch dynamically between Grouped Bar, Multi-Series Line, or Donut/Pie distributions.
  • Interactive Calendar: Step backward and forward through history or pick specific calendar dates to inspect historical incidents.
Weekly Report (7 Days)
4. Weekly Positive vs. Negative Balance Graph

When viewing the Weekly Report (7 Days), a dedicated dual-bar balance graph compares daily communication factors:

🟢 Green Bar (Positive / Collective Factor)

Points accumulated from collective identifiers ("we", "team", "together"), constructive tone, and autonomy support.

🔴 Orange/Red Bar (Negative / Pressure Factor)

Points accumulated from self-centered pronouns ("I", "me", "my"), urgency pressure, ultimatums, and manipulation.

Actionable Target: The green bar should consistently outweigh the red bar each day. If the red bar spikes on a particular day, hover over the bar to inspect message volume and balance ratio, then review that day's communications to de-escalate tension.

Academic Framework

The 5 Dimensions of Exploitative Leadership (ELTC)

The platform assesses communication using the five validated dimensions established in organizational behavior research by Schmid, Verdorfer, and Peus:

01
Genuine Egoistic Behaviors
Self-Credit & Self-Interest

Prioritizing the leader's personal status, credit, and self-promotion above the well-being, workload, and psychological safety of subordinates.

❌ Problematic: "I completed this project myself and I achieved outstanding results for my organization."
✅ Constructive: "Our team completed this project together and achieved outstanding milestones for our organization."
02
Taking Credit
Appropriating Output

Claiming personal ownership of accomplishments, ideas, or work delivered by team members without giving appropriate attribution.

❌ Problematic: "I resolved the client escalation and redesigned the entire system over the weekend."
✅ Constructive: "Thanks to the dedication of our engineering team, we resolved the client escalation together."
03
Exerting Pressure
Intimidation & Ultimatums

Relying on anxiety, artificial urgency, implied penalties, or aggressive ultimatums to drive compliance and accelerate output.

❌ Problematic: "Get this finished by 5 PM today or don't bother showing up tomorrow."
✅ Constructive: "Our target is 5 PM today. Please let me know early if you encounter blockers so we can support you."
04
Undermining Development
Autonomy Restriction

Suppressing junior initiative, discouraging independent thought, micromanaging execution, or keeping subordinates dependent.

❌ Problematic: "Just stick to the instructions given; you don't have enough seniority to question our process."
✅ Constructive: "I value your initiative in reviewing this process. Let's discuss your proposal together in our next sync."
05
Manipulating Followers
Emotional Coercion

Using guilt-tripping, personal obligations, or emotional leverage to coerce extra work or extract compliance.

❌ Problematic: "After everything I've done for your career, the least you could do is take on this weekend workload."
✅ Constructive: "We have an unexpected weekend coverage need. Let's discuss as a team who has capacity or how we can balance it."
