ToxicRadar Communication Quality Score
======================================

Purpose
-------
The Total History trend graph shows how the quality of analyzed email communication changes over time.
Higher values mean more respectful and autonomy-supportive communication. Lower values mean stronger
toxic or exploitative signals.

The score is designed as a continuous trend measure, not as a simple positive/negative percentage.
This is useful for scientific analysis because it preserves severity information from the underlying
dimension ratings.


Input Scores
------------
Each analyzed email contains five behavioral dimension scores:

1. Genuine Egoistic Behaviors
2. Taking Credit
3. Exerting Pressure
4. Undermining Development
5. Manipulating

Each dimension is scored from 1 to 5:

1 = minimal / neutral or constructive
2 = low / minor or ambiguous signal
3 = moderate / probable exploitative tone
4 = high / clear exploitative behavior
5 = very high / explicit exploitative tone

Important: In the raw dimension scores, higher means worse.

For statistical consistency, ToxicRadar stores all five dimension scores for every analyzed email.
This also applies when a dimension has score 1. Score-1 dimensions may be omitted from a compact
human-readable table, but they must still exist in the machine-readable analysis record and in the
Excel tracking columns.

If an older Excel record does not contain one of the five dimensions, ToxicRadar treats that missing
dimension as score 1 for backward-compatible dashboard statistics.


Email-Level Formula
-------------------
For each email, ToxicRadar first computes the mean toxicity across the five dimensions:

dimensionToxicityScore =
  (score_1 + score_2 + score_3 + score_4 + score_5) / 5

Then the score is inverted and normalized to a 0-100 communication quality scale:

communicationQualityScore =
  ((5 - dimensionToxicityScore) / 4) * 100

The result is clamped to the range 0-100 and rounded to the nearest integer.


Scale Interpretation
--------------------
dimensionToxicityScore = 1 -> communicationQualityScore = 100
dimensionToxicityScore = 2 -> communicationQualityScore = 75
dimensionToxicityScore = 3 -> communicationQualityScore = 50
dimensionToxicityScore = 4 -> communicationQualityScore = 25
dimensionToxicityScore = 5 -> communicationQualityScore = 0

Interpretation:

81-100 = strong / highly constructive communication
61-80  = generally respectful communication
41-60  = mixed communication quality
21-40  = high-risk communication
0-20   = critical communication quality


Time-Level Formula
------------------
The graph groups emails over time.

For each time period, the graph computes the average of all email-level communication quality scores:

periodCommunicationQuality =
  (qualityEmail_1 + qualityEmail_2 + ... + qualityEmail_n) / n

Example:

If January contains 13 emails and their quality scores are:
100, 100, 95, 90, ...

then January is the average of those 13 values.


Time Buckets
------------
The graph automatically chooses the grouping:

- Daily buckets for short histories up to about 45 days.
- Monthly buckets for longer histories.

This keeps the trend readable while still preserving change over time.


Why Overall Toxicity Is Not Used In This Trend Score
----------------------------------------------------
The graph does not combine Overall Toxicity with the dimension average.

Reason:
Overall Toxicity is usually derived from, or strongly related to, the dimension scores.
Combining it again with the dimension average can double-count the same information.

For a cleaner statistical trend, the graph uses the mean of the five dimension scores only.


Why Overall Flag Is Not Used In This Trend Score
------------------------------------------------
Overall Flag is not subtracted from the quality score.

Reason:
The flag is a threshold-based indicator, while the graph is a continuous severity trend.
Mixing a threshold penalty into the score would make the metric harder to interpret
statistically.

Overall Flag can still be analyzed separately as a rate:

flagRate =
  flaggedEmails / totalEmails * 100


Difference From Positive Rate
-----------------------------
A positive-rate graph would use:

positiveRate =
  positiveEmails / totalEmails * 100

That is easier to explain but less informative for scientific trend analysis.
It treats a slightly problematic email and a highly problematic email too similarly.

The Communication Quality Score keeps severity information, which makes trend changes
more meaningful.
